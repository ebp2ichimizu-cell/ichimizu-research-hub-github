# Factors Associated with Continued Police Action

> **About this commentary**  
> This page is an independent Research Hub commentary based on publicly available source material. It is not an explanation by the original authors. Please consult the original source for the exact study methods, results and authors’ interpretations.

## Studying implementation behavior inside a police organization

Nara Prefectural Police introduced an internal award to encourage officers to promote applications for suspension of unused international telephone services as a special-fraud countermeasure.

Application-promotion counts increased approximately twentyfold after the award program was introduced, but performance varied widely across officers.

The study therefore asked a different question:

> **Among officers who had already received an award, what distinguished those who continued the activity from those who did not?**

A survey was sent to 281 award recipients; 182 responded.

---

## What was measured?

Fifteen four-point items were initially grouped into:

- intrinsic motivation;
- efficacy;
- skill/habit;
- extrinsic motivation; and
- organizational support.

Among respondents:

- 151 continued the activity;
- 31 did not.

The study then used group comparisons, regression analyses, and exploratory factor analysis.

## Check the statistics

### Respondents

- award recipients: 281
- responses: 182
- approximate response rate: 64.8%
- continuation group: 151
- non-continuation group: 31

### Group differences

Skill/habit:

- t=2.24
- p=.026
- d=.442

Extrinsic motivation:

- t=2.28
- p=.024
- d=.450

Both scores were higher in the continuation group.

These are moderate-to-small standardized differences, not deterministic separation between groups.

### Regression results

When multiple scales were considered together, the paper reports that the scale scores did **not** have statistically significant independent effects on continuation status or post-continuation promotion volume.

This matters because a significant t-test difference does not automatically imply an independent predictor.

### Exploratory factor analysis

Fourteen items were re-examined using promax rotation, producing three factors:

1. resident response / achievement experience;
2. feedback / monitoring;
3. organizational framework / goal setting.

Officers reporting positive resident responses and achievement experiences tended to report stronger continuation intentions and higher post-continuation activity levels.

The analysis was exploratory rather than confirmatory.

### The “20-fold increase”

The approximately twentyfold increase occurred after introduction of the award program.

There was no randomized or concurrent untreated comparison.

The evidence therefore supports:

> activity increased sharply after program introduction,

not:

> the award caused a twentyfold increase.

## How should the findings be interpreted?

The study suggests that implementation may depend on more than motivation alone.

Continued delivery may be supported by:

- practical skill;
- habitual integration into routine work;
- visible achievement;
- resident feedback;
- monitoring; and
- organizational goals.

The study does not estimate the causal effect of the award itself.

---

## Limitations

- award recipients only;
- post-intervention self-report;
- small non-continuation group;
- no randomized award comparison;
- one-page conference abstract limits statistical detail.

---

## Research Hub commentary

Evidence-based policing depends on **implementation behavior by police officers**, not only on citizen behavior.

A useful implementation pathway is:

**evidence-based tactic → officer adoption → repeated delivery → organizational routine → citizen reach → outcome.**

If delivery stops after a short campaign, population impact remains limited.

The practical lesson is therefore to design not only incentives, but also **skills, feedback, monitoring and opportunities for achievement**.

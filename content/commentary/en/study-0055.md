# Poster Experiment for Preventing SNS Investment Fraud in Private Workplaces

> **About this commentary**  
> This page is an independent commentary prepared by the Research Hub to help police practitioners and crime-prevention professionals understand the study and its evidential limits. It is not an explanation provided or endorsed by the original authors. Please consult the original source for the exact study details.

Fifteen supermarkets in Osaka were randomly assigned to three poster conditions for 14 days.

<!-- STATS_DETAIL_START -->

## Check the statistics

615 employees completed both waves. Only 234 (**38%**) noticed the poster. The exposure-aware analysis used 156 participants who noticed and recognized an intervention poster.

Threat appraisal showed no significant group, time, or interaction effects. Across groups and waves, 93% or more selected “do nothing” for a simulated fraud advertisement.

The 156-person analysis conditions on intervention exposure and should not be treated as the population-average effect.

<!-- STATS_DETAIL_END -->

## Research Hub commentary

The main finding is an implementation bottleneck: **installed → noticed → remembered → understood → behavior**. Only 38% reached the noticing stage.

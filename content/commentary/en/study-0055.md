# Poster Experiment for Preventing SNS Investment Fraud in Private Workplaces

> **About this commentary**  
> This page is an independent commentary prepared by the Research Hub to help police practitioners and crime-prevention professionals understand the study and its evidential limits. It is not an explanation provided or endorsed by the original authors. Please consult the original source for the exact study details.

Fifteen supermarkets were randomly assigned to three poster conditions for 14 days.

The key finding was not a strong message effect. It was an **implementation bottleneck**: only 38% of employees noticed the poster.

<!-- STATS_DETAIL_START -->

## Check the statistics

615 employees completed both waves.

- noticed the poster: 234, **38%**
- main exposure-aware analysis: 156 participants

Threat appraisal showed no significant group, time, or interaction effects.

At every group and time point, **93% or more** selected “do nothing” when shown a simulated investment-fraud advertisement.

The 156-person analysis conditions on noticing/recognizing the intervention, so it should not be treated as the population-average treatment effect.

<!-- STATS_DETAIL_END -->

## Research Hub commentary

The intervention pathway was:

**poster installed → noticed → understood → remembered → behavior.**

Most participants did not reach the second stage.

The next experiment should improve visibility, placement and exposure before optimizing message wording.

## Key takeaway

Before asking whether a nudge worked, ask whether the intervention was actually seen.

# Community Safety Mapping with a Crime-Prevention App

> **About this commentary**  
> This page is an independent Research Hub commentary based on publicly available source material. It is not an explanation by the original authors. Please consult the original source for the exact study methods, results and authors’ interpretations.

## Teaching participants to read risky places rather than identify “suspicious people”

This research series developed and applied a mobile crime-prevention walking app for community safety mapping.

Participants walked through neighborhoods, recorded risky and safe places, attached reasons and photographs, discussed the locations in groups, and proposed local prevention improvements.

The series progressed from a university course to:

- junior-high and university students;
- general community seminar participants;
- Girl Scouts and after-school child-club participants.

The central educational idea is opportunity-focused crime prevention: analyze **places and environmental conditions**, not personal appearance or “suspiciousness.”

## Check the statistics

### study-0042: junior-high students

Pre/post responses were initially obtained from 77 students.

Eight records could not be confidently matched across waves because of duplicate attendance numbers or related identification issues.

Final analysis:
- **N=69**
- 31 boys
- 38 girls

Students worked in groups of four or five using tablets.

Crime-prevention ability was assessed with 24 items across four factors:

1. victimization prevention;
2. communication;
3. place attachment;
4. delinquency prevention.

Crime-prevention awareness used 22 items across five factors:

1. outdoor prevention awareness;
2. suspicion/caution toward strangers;
3. home prevention awareness;
4. risk-avoidance behavior;
5. self-protection awareness.

The study reports improvement in prevention ability and awareness after the activity.

### University sample

- **N=27**

A comparable app-supported mapping activity was implemented.

### study-0044

- Girl Scouts: 21
- after-school child club: 18
- total: **39**

The study reported improved crime-prevention ability and high activity ratings.

### Why exact t statistics are not reproduced

The current verified source record supports the sample sizes, procedure, scale structure and direction of results, but not a complete statistical table that can be transcribed safely here.

A later 2024 conference study reported detailed t statistics for a subsequent improved program, but those values belong to a different follow-up study and are not transferred to study-0042.

### Can this be called “the app effect”?

Not cleanly.

The intervention included:

**pre-learning + fieldwork + app recording + group discussion + proposal development + presentation + feedback.**

There was no randomized paper-map comparison.

The evidence therefore supports the effectiveness of the **app-supported mapping program**, not a causal effect of the app by itself.

## Research Hub commentary

The main educational mechanism is not map production.

It is a reasoning process:

**observe a place → explain why it may be risky or safe → compare interpretations → identify environmental conditions → propose improvement.**

This aligns with crime opportunity theory, CPTED, and basic problem-oriented thinking.

The series is also useful as an implementation sequence: the program was repeatedly adapted to different populations rather than being tested once and treated as universally established.

## Limitations

- primarily pre/post designs without untreated control groups;
- self-reported ability and awareness;
- multi-component intervention, so the app cannot be isolated;
- small samples in some extensions;
- no direct victimization outcome.

## Key takeaway

Community safety mapping should be evaluated as a **problem-analysis learning process**, not merely as a digital mapping product.

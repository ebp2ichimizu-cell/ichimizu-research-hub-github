# Examining the Effect of CCTV in Downtown Areas

> **About this commentary**  
> This page is an independent commentary prepared by the Research Hub to help police practitioners and crime-prevention professionals understand the study and its evidential limits. It is not an explanation provided or endorsed by the original authors. Please consult the original source for the exact study details.

The study evaluated CCTV in Hakata-Nakasu and Tenjin, using one-year pre/post crime data and Kokura Station South as a comparison area. Outcomes included violent crime, purse snatching, bicycle theft and vehicle-related crime.

The analysis used a Difference-in-Differences-style **Net Effect (NE)** and a **Weighted Displacement Quotient (WDQ)**.

## Main findings

Hakata-Nakasu showed relative reductions for purse snatching, bicycle theft and vehicle-related crime, but not violent crime. The strongest NE occurred at about 260 m, 300 m and 200 m respectively. Tenjin did not show comparable reductions.

A key difference was placement: CCTV in Hakata-Nakasu aligned more closely with pre-existing crime concentrations.

## Check the statistics

At 100 m in Hakata-Nakasu:

- purse snatching: 6 → 4, NE 0.44
- bicycle theft: 70 → 56, NE 0.95
- vehicle-related crime: 14 → 15, NE 0.04

Purse snatching showed mild displacement, bicycle theft showed diffusion of benefits in the examined buffer, and vehicle-related crime showed some displacement that did not outweigh the intervention-area effect.

This was not a randomized trial; it compared one treated area with one comparison area.

## Research Hub commentary

The key lesson is not simply “CCTV works.” It is:

**crime analysis → targeted placement → comparative evaluation → displacement check.**

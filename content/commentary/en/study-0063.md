# Bicycle-Theft Risk at Public Parking Facilities
## Associations can change when site context and confounding are addressed

> **About this commentary**  
> This page is an independent Research Hub commentary based on the cited source material. It distinguishes reported findings, authors’ interpretations and Research Hub commentary, and should be read alongside the original source.


The study series examined **417 public bicycle parking facilities** around railway stations in Saitama Prefecture and assigned **5,172 thefts over 66 months** to those facilities.

It used multilevel negative-binomial models and propensity-score matching to distinguish simple facility correlations from more adjusted comparisons.

<!-- STATS_DETAIL_START -->

## Check the statistics

Selected basic-model coefficients:

- bicycle-parking building: -1.09, p<.001
- underground facility: -1.50, p<.001
- roadside: .41, p<.05
- temporary-use: 1.04, p<.01
- free parking: 1.12, p<.001
- gate access: -.92, p<.001
- attendant: -.33, p<.05

After adjusting for each station's share of theft occurring in public parking, several associations weakened:

- building: -.97, p<.001
- underground: -1.36, p<.001
- temporary use: 1.15, p<.01
- gate: -1.00, p<.001
- free parking: .00, not significant
- attendant: -.08, not significant

Propensity-score matching:

- CCTV: ATE -12.1, 95%CI [-18.5,-6.6]
- attendant: ATE 3.6, [-1.8,9.4]
- free parking: ATE 21.5, [11.8,33.5]

These are observational adjusted differences, not randomized treatment effects.

## How should the findings be interpreted?

The study is valuable because conclusions change as confounding is addressed.

“Attendant present” looks protective in simpler models but not in propensity matching. That is not failure; it is exactly why adjusted analyses are needed.

The remaining hypotheses—CCTV, access control, charging structure and temporary use—should be tested prospectively.

## Research Hub commentary

Observational risk factors should not be converted directly into a prevention checklist.

A stronger EBP sequence is:

**identify association → test robustness → select a modifiable factor → intervene prospectively → measure theft.**

## Limitations

- observational design;
- actual bicycle turnover was not fully measured;
- locking rate was not a principal covariate;
- unmeasured confounding remains;
- displacement was not tested.

## Key takeaway

Use the analysis to select the **next intervention to test**, not to claim causal treatment effects.

# Kagawa Shoplifting Prevention EBP Series

> **About this commentary**  
> This page is an independent Research Hub commentary based on publicly available source material. It is not an explanation by the original authors. Please consult the original source for the exact study methods, results and authors’ interpretations.

## A long-running evidence-to-practice cycle

This body of work linked Kagawa University, Kagawa Prefectural Police and retail/community partners.

Its importance lies less in one treatment effect than in the repeated cycle:

**diagnose the problem → collect evidence → develop a practical tool → implement → evaluate use and response → revise.**

Early studies surveyed stores and security practice. Later work created and distributed shoplifting-prevention manuals, developed proactive staff-greeting guidance, compared training formats, expanded to youth/parent education, and eventually linked crime prevention with hospitality and community CSR.

## Check the statistics

Selected studies illustrate different stages of the cycle.

### Problem analysis
study-0005:
- 84 store managers;
- stores classified into no/low/high shoplifting groups;
- store characteristics, responses, preventive measures and manager attitudes compared.

### Implementation
study-0004:
- nearly half of stores reportedly displayed or used the manual for staff education;
- roughly 90% evaluated the initiative positively.

### Practice-cycle redesign
study-0007:
- store and security-guard research informed a new proactive greeting manual;
- the manual was trialed in stores;
- effectiveness was reported as suggestive rather than definitive.

### Training format
study-0008:
- in-person training: 162 participants;
- prevention video: 42;
- the training group reported higher overall evaluation and stronger sense of practical prevention.

### Community partnership
study-0014:
- 130 neighborhood-watch volunteers;
- 90 store CSR-training participants;
- both groups rated the training and perceived activity meaning highly.

### Staff education
study-0017:
- 51 staff;
- 11 managers.

Hospitality changes included:
- service provision: t(45)=2.658, p<.05
- welcoming behavior: t(45)=2.504, p<.05
- customer understanding: t(46)=2.449, p<.05

These studies are heterogeneous and cannot be pooled into a single causal effect estimate.

## Research Hub commentary

The series is best understood as an implementation history of EBP:

**evidence → practice → feedback → adaptation.**

Its evidence base includes surveys, pre/post evaluations and implementation studies rather than a sequence of randomized trials.

The strongest transferable lesson is therefore methodological: build a system that repeatedly measures the problem and revises the intervention.

## Key takeaway

A prevention manual is not the end product. The meaningful endpoint is a cycle in which practice generates new evidence and the intervention is updated.

# Improving Bicycle Locking in Large Residential Parking Areas

> **About this commentary**  
> This page is an independent Research Hub commentary based on publicly available source material. It is not an explanation by the original authors. Please consult the original source for the exact study methods, results and authors’ interpretations.

## Comparing wall posters with tags attached directly to bicycles

This field experiment was conducted in bicycle parking areas serving **18 large apartment buildings in Nishinomiya, Hyogo**.

Buildings were assigned to five conditions: no intervention, conventional poster, nudge poster, Shikake tag, or poster-plus-tag.

Locking was observed before intervention and one, two and four weeks later.

The central finding is temporal: poster-based conditions improved earlier, while the tag condition showed its largest increase at four weeks.

## Check the statistics

Baseline:
- 2,831 bicycles
- 216 unlocked
- locking rate **92.4%**

Overall post-intervention locking:
- week 1: 93.5%
- week 2: 93.4%
- week 4: 93.1%

Change from baseline:

| Condition | Week 1 | Week 4 |
|---|---:|---:|
| Combined | +1.7 pp | +0.6 pp |
| Nudge poster | +1.4 | +0.5 |
| Conventional poster | +0.8 | +0.3 |
| Shikake tag | +0.4 | **+2.8** |

At the final observation, 358 bicycles (12.6%) were classified as abandoned/long-term parked; their locking rate was 77.1%.

After excluding them:

| Condition | Week 1 | Week 4 |
|---|---:|---:|
| Combined | **+2.0 pp** | +1.1 |
| Nudge poster | +1.7 | +0.6 |
| Conventional poster | +1.3 | +0.6 |
| Shikake tag | +0.4 | **+2.9** |

The authors report a statistically significant improvement for the Shikake-tag condition at four weeks, but the paper does not provide the corresponding test statistic or exact p-value.

## Research Hub commentary

The EBP lesson is to measure the **trajectory** of an intervention.

A quick-acting message may fade, while a persistent environmental cue may emerge more slowly.

The study does not establish that tags are universally superior or that bicycle theft itself was reduced.

## Key takeaway

Ask not only which intervention works, but **how quickly, for how long, and under what site conditions**.

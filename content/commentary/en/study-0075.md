# Can Directing Customer Flow and Smiles Reduce Shoplifting?
## Separating changes in flow, smiling, atmosphere and actual crime

> **About this commentary**  
> This series commentary uses peer-reviewed study-0075 as the representative study and incorporates the earlier conference report study-0074. It distinguishes quantitatively measured intermediate outcomes from store managers' perceptions of shoplifting change.

---

## What was the intervention idea?

The project identified in-store shoplifting hot spots and attempted to increase natural presence and interaction around them. Smile-recognition/donation tablets, staff movement and smiling, and promotion by the humanoid robot Pepper were used to increase customer flow, communication and hospitality.

A home centre and a discount store participated, with eleven hot-spot locations identified in each store.

---

## From study-0074 to study-0075

Study-0074 was a conference abstract reporting increased flow and improved atmosphere in the discount-store experiment. Study-0075 provides the fuller peer-reviewed account, combining interviews with two store managers and quantitative flow/smile data.

The Hub therefore treats study-0075 as the series representative and links study-0074 here.

---

## What changed?

At the discount store, Pepper was not functioning during the first week and was used during weeks two to four. This accidental pre-period allowed a before/after analysis.

Across 28 days at the hot spots, the study recorded:

- **41,296 passers-by**; and
- **3,448 smiles**.

Pepper installation was associated with increased flow. In a model adjusting for flow, Pepper installation was also positively associated with the rate of smiling; exponentiating the coefficient was interpreted as roughly a **2.8-fold** increase in smile occurrence compared with the pre-installation period.

Manager interviews suggested increases in communication, hospitality and store atmosphere. Perceptions of shoplifting differed by store: the home-centre manager reported a reduction in severe incidents, whereas the discount-store manager did not report a reduction in shoplifting.

## Check the statistics

The 28-day discount-store dataset included 168 observations across tablet locations, 41,296 customer passages and 3,448 smiles.

For smiles, a model using log flow as an offset reported a Pepper-installation coefficient of **B = 1.03, SE = 0.44, p < .05**, corresponding to an exponentiated value of about 2.80.

This is an effect on the measured **smile rate**, not a 2.8-fold reduction in shoplifting.

<!-- STATS_DETAIL_END -->

## Read the mechanism as a chain

hot-spot identification → attract people → increase presence and interaction → increase smiling/hospitality → alter atmosphere and guardianship → influence offending decisions → reduce shoplifting.

The study provides relatively direct evidence for the earlier links, especially flow and smiles. The final crime-reduction link is much less secure because it relies largely on qualitative manager reports and differs between the two stores.

---

## Limitations

Only two stores were studied and their corporate approaches differed. There was no randomised control condition. The intervention combined several components, making their separate contributions difficult to identify. Objective shoplifting counts or loss values were not the main quantitative outcome.

---

# Research Hub commentary: create presence rather than simply intensify surveillance

The practical innovation is to increase natural visibility through customer service and social activity rather than relying only on suspicious-person surveillance. This may align crime prevention with customer experience, but a full evaluation still needs shoplifting rates, loss, staff burden and customer outcomes.

---

## Follow-up

A second Kagawa University–Kagawa Prefectural Police experiment using a smaller humanoid robot began in November 2026. Results are not yet available, so it should be treated as an ongoing follow-up rather than evidence of effectiveness.

---

## Take-away

**Do not treat increased flow and smiles as equivalent to reduced shoplifting.**

The study demonstrates changes in intermediate implementation outcomes. Stronger evidence is still needed for the final crime and loss outcomes.

# Promoting Bicycle Locking with Gratitude Messages

> **About this commentary**  
> This page is an independent Research Hub commentary based on publicly available source material. It is not an explanation by the original authors. Please consult the original source for the exact study methods, results and authors’ interpretations.


This pilot study tested whether gratitude-based messaging could promote bicycle locking at three public bicycle parking areas.

The theoretical move was important: instead of relying only on threat appeals, the study explored **reciprocity and positive social framing**.

Actual locking behavior was observed before installation and at one and two months after installation.

## Check the statistics

The publicly accessible conference program confirms:

- 3 public bicycle parking areas;
- 3 sign-message conditions;
- observations at baseline, one month and two months;
- actual locking behavior as the outcome.

The underlying one-page result table could not be safely re-verified in the current source environment, so exact locking rates, p-values and effect sizes are not reproduced.

Current Research Hub records indicate heterogeneous directions across the three messages. Because later studies summarize this research stream more broadly, the present page does not turn the pilot into a definitive ranking of message types.

## Research Hub commentary

The main contribution is hypothesis generation.

**move beyond threat appeals → test gratitude/reciprocity framing → observe heterogeneous results → expand to larger multi-site experiments.**

This study is best read as a precursor to the later Fukushima 14-site experiment rather than a final answer about the “best” message.

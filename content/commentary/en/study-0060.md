# Anti-Voyeurism Mirror Research Series

> **About this commentary**  
> This page is an independent Research Hub commentary based on publicly available source material. It is not an explanation by the original authors. Please consult the original source for the exact study methods, results and authors’ interpretations.

## From field experiment to implementation and follow-up

This series examines whether mirrors placed near upward escalators can naturally induce passengers to look behind or to the side, thereby creating a setting in which covert filming may be perceived as more detectable.

The key feature is the progression:

**mechanism test → field implementation → scale-up → long-term follow-up.**

### study-0060: mechanism test

Sixteen escalators at four JR stations in Saitama were assigned to mirror-only, poster-only, mirror-plus-poster, or no-intervention conditions. Mirror size was also varied.

Before intervention, only 19 of 1,518 observed users (1.3%) looked back.

After intervention, some conditions reached roughly 20%.

## Check the statistics

The regression analysis used N=2,976 observations.

Immediately after intervention:

- poster: B=.047, p=.13
- small mirror: B=.017, p=.59
- **large mirror: B=.174, p<.01**
- poster × large mirror: B=-.092, p=.07

One week later:

- poster: B=.011, p=.72
- small mirror: B=.054, p=.08
- **large mirror: B=.081, p=.04**
- poster × large mirror: B=.034, p=.50

Large mirrors therefore produced the clearest and most persistent increase in looking behavior.

The outcome is **looking behavior**, not voyeurism incidents.

### study-0053: implementation

Kyoto Prefectural Police installed mirrors at 18 locations across 10 stations, with behavioral observation at 8 locations across 6 stations.

One month after installation, looking behavior was reported to be approximately **four times** the pre-installation level.

The program was then expanded to **28 stations and 41 locations** by the end of 2025.

### study-0054: persistence

The follow-up study tracked the same policy intervention over short-term points and at 6 and 12 months. Research Hub records indicate approximately 2,400 observations, with the increase broadly persisting but with heterogeneous attenuation by age and sex.

Detailed coefficients are not reproduced here because the one-page conference source does not provide enough verified detail for safe transcription.

## Research Hub commentary

The most important lesson is not that “mirrors work.”

It is the EBP/EBPM sequence:

**test a behavioral mechanism → identify the workable component → implement → evaluate → scale → check persistence.**

The studies do not yet establish a quantified reduction in voyeurism itself. They establish change in a plausible intermediate behavior.

## Key takeaway

Environmental cues can sometimes outperform information-heavy warnings in fast-moving environments, but the final crime outcome still needs to be measured.

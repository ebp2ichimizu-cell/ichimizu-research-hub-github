# Spatio-Temporal Clustering of Apartment Burglary
## Risk changes after a burglary; it is not spatially static

> **About this commentary**  
> This page is an independent Research Hub commentary based on the cited source material. It distinguishes reported findings, authors’ interpretations and Research Hub commentary, and should be read alongside the original source.


The study analyzed **8,845 apartment burglaries in Fukuoka City from 2005–2014** to examine repeat victimization, near repeats, building repeats, and building near repeats.

The purpose was analytical rather than intervention-based: identify when and where short-term risk increases enough to justify targeted patrol or communication.

## Check the statistics

Building-level analysis:
- N=8,118
- 31% of incidents were in building-repeat sequences
- 27% of victimized buildings were victimized again
- about 1.6% of buildings accounted for about 8.3% of incidents

Building-repeat timing:
- 16% within 24 hours
- 32% within 30 days
- 37% within 60 days
- 59% within 360 days

Near-repeat analysis found elevated risk across many cells within **300 m and 70 days**.

Knox ratios in days 0–10:
- same building: 23.1
- 1–100 m: 4.5
- 101–200 m: 2.3
- 201–300 m: 1.9

Unit-level analysis:
- N=6,661
- repeat incidents: 560 (8.4%)
- 6.9% of victimized units were victimized again

Days 0–10:
- repeat: 18.3
- building near repeat: 24.2

These are observed-to-expected ratios, not absolute probability multipliers.

## Research Hub commentary

The practical value is dynamic targeting.

A burglary can trigger a temporary risk window for the same building and nearby locations. This supports testing interventions such as rapid cocoon neighborhood watch, short-term patrol, and targeted security checks.

The study does **not** show that those interventions reduce burglary; that would require a separate impact evaluation.

## Limitations

The data are observational, historical and depend on recorded offence times and locations. The analysis also does not establish that repeat events were committed by the same offender.

## Key takeaway

Use new incidents to update **where and for how long** prevention resources are concentrated.

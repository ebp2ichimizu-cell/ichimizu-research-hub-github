# The Effect of Neighborhood Crime Prevention on Public Perceptions of Crime

> **About this commentary**  
> This page is an independent Research Hub commentary based on publicly available source material. It is not an explanation by the original authors. Please consult the original source for the exact study methods, results and authors’ interpretations.

## Visible prevention can change risk perception without changing fear in the same way

A survey of **1,171 residents** in a metropolitan municipality examined recognition of two visible prevention measures:

- blue-light community patrols;
- public crime-map displays.

The study separated four outcomes:

- perceived victimization risk;
- emotional fear of crime;
- neighborhood safety evaluation;
- neighborhood satisfaction.

This distinction is the study's main conceptual contribution.

## Check the statistics

Perceived-risk means by patrol recognition:

- unaware: 8.17
- knowledge only: 8.57
- saw patrol vehicle: **9.16**

ANOVA:
**F(2,1109)=4.91, p<.01**

After demographic and contextual controls, the hierarchical regression estimated approximately:

- +0.53 points for knowing about the patrol;
- +0.89 points for having seen the patrol vehicle.

Crime-map recognition was related to neighborhood satisfaction:

- unaware: 8.50
- knowledge only: 8.14
- saw display: 8.86
- checked content: **9.47**

ANOVA:
**F(3,1121)=4.09, p<.01**

Fear of crime did not show the same pattern.

This is an observational survey, not a randomized intervention. Exposure to prevention measures may be endogenous to local crime, concern, or civic engagement.

## Research Hub commentary

The practical lesson is that “reassurance” should not be measured with one item.

Visible prevention may simultaneously:

- signal guardianship;
- make crime risk more salient.

Risk perception, fear, neighborhood evaluation and satisfaction can therefore move differently.

## Key takeaway

Evaluate community-prevention communication with multiple psychological outcomes, not a single “sense of safety” measure.

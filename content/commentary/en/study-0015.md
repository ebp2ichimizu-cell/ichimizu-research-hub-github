# Sexual-Offence Locations and Perceived Risk
## Places that feel dangerous do not fully overlap with places where offences occurred

> **About this commentary**  
> This page is an independent Research Hub commentary based on the cited source material. It distinguishes reported findings, authors’ interpretations and Research Hub commentary, and should be read alongside the original source.


The study combined police-recorded sexual-offence locations, environmental field observation, and risk-perception mapping among female university students.

Across 142 recorded incidents:
- 54% occurred between 21:00 and 02:00;
- 66% occurred on roads;
- 82% involved victims travelling on foot.

Twenty-eight university-area locations were inspected in detail.

## Check the statistics

Among the 28 locations:
- 17 (61%) were below 1.0 lux;
- 17 (61%) were on roads with one lane or less;
- 11 (39%) were on two-or-more-lane roads.

Students' reasons for identifying risky places:
- dark: 88%
- few people: 63%
- narrow: 17%

Of 20 offence locations represented on the survey map:
- only 8 overlapped exactly with perceived-risk locations;
- 12 (60%) were not identified as risky.

Importantly, 8 incidents (29%) occurred at locations with at least 3.0 lux, and 11 (39%) occurred on wider roads.

## How should the findings be interpreted?

The result does not mean students were “wrong.” Darkness and lack of people were genuinely common features.

The problem is that **single-cue heuristics** miss risk that emerges from combinations of time, activity, guardianship and environmental conditions.

The study also lacks pedestrian-exposure denominators, so incident counts are not individual risk rates.

## Research Hub commentary

This makes the study useful for CPTED-oriented prevention education.

“Bright” does not necessarily mean “well supervised,” and environmental advice should be updated using actual incident locations rather than subjective fear alone.

## Limitations

- observational analysis rather than intervention;
- unreported sexual offences are absent from police data;
- no pedestrian exposure denominator;
- risk perception sample limited to female university students.

## Key takeaway

Compare subjective danger maps with **actual incident and environmental data** before designing safety advice.

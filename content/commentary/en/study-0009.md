# Effects of a Community Crime-Prevention Program on Perceived Public Safety and Fear of Crime

> **About this commentary**  
> This page is an independent commentary prepared by the Research Hub to help police practitioners and crime-prevention professionals understand the study and its evidential limits. It is not an explanation provided or endorsed by the original authors. Please consult the original source for the exact study details.

The study examined Chiba Prefecture's convenience-store crime-prevention box program using pre/post resident surveys at two sites.

Perceived neighborhood safety generally improved, but some measures of perceived crime risk and fear were higher among residents with greater exposure to the intervention.

<!-- STATS_DETAIL_START -->

## Check the statistics

Valid responses:
- Chiba: 762 pre, 728 post
- Ichikawa: 619 pre, 605 post

“Safety improved”:
- Chiba 3.7% → 7.6%
- Ichikawa 3.9% → 5.8%

In Chiba after implementation, residents within a 10-minute walk reported higher perceived-risk scores (9.71 vs 8.79, p=.009) and fear-of-crime scores (10.28 vs 9.52, p=.044).

These associations should not be interpreted as clean causal side effects because exposure was not randomly assigned.

<!-- STATS_DETAIL_END -->

## Research Hub commentary

Visible prevention can plausibly produce reassurance and heightened awareness of risk at the same time. Program evaluation should measure them separately.

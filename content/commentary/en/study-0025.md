# Promoting Bicycle Locking at a Commercial Facility

> **About this commentary**  
> This page is an independent Research Hub commentary based on publicly available source material. It is not an explanation by the original authors. Please consult the original source for the exact study methods, results and authors’ interpretations.

## Comparing statistics + social norms, regret framing, and an eye cue

Three bicycle-parking areas at a large commercial facility in Hikone, Shiga, received different A1 signs:

- statistics + social norm;
- statistics + anticipated-regret framing;
- an eye image with a short locking message.

Actual bicycle locking was repeatedly observed before and after installation.

The site was substantively relevant: 17.1% of Hikone bicycle thefts in 2022 and 16.5% during January–May 2023 occurred at the facility.

## Check the statistics

### Statistics + social norm

Baseline:
- locked 247/277 = **89.2%**

Post:
- locked 368/393 = **93.6%**

χ²(1)=4.31, p=.045

Logistic regression:
- B=.58
- p=.04
- OR=1.79
- 95%CI [1.03, 3.11]

### Statistics + regret

Baseline:
- 258/299 = **86.3%**

Post:
- 147/174 = **84.5%**

χ²(1)=.29, p=.59

OR=.87, 95%CI [.51, 1.46]

### Eye cue

Baseline:
- 262/302 = **86.8%**

Post:
- 298/359 = **83.0%**

χ²(1)=1.78, p=.18

OR=.75, 95%CI [.48, 1.15]

Total observations: **1,804 bicycle observations**.

### Critical design limitation

Each message was fixed to one parking area. Message and site were therefore confounded.

The study also did not measure whether users actually noticed, read, or remembered the sign.

The positive result for the first condition therefore supports the combined intervention at that site; it does not isolate the causal contribution of statistics versus social norms.

## Research Hub commentary

The useful conclusion is not simply “social norms won.”

The study identifies the next design problem:

**message content → site context → exposure → recognition → locking behavior.**

Later multi-site studies in the same research stream address several of these limitations.

## Key takeaway

A small field experiment is most useful when it generates a better next experiment rather than a universal message ranking.
